## A chain of pressures

Seasonal sugarcane work brings together drought, debt, migration and informal labour. Women work long hours while managing cooking, water, childcare and temporary living conditions. Limited sanitation and interrupted access to healthcare turn ordinary health needs into workplace risks.

Reports of high hysterectomy prevalence in Maharashtra's sugar belt brought public attention to the issue. But focusing only on surgery can obscure the structure around it: recruitment practices, poor accommodation, weak social protection and health systems that are difficult for migrant workers to navigate.

> Health is shaped long before a worker reaches a clinic.

## Research that connects systems

I brought together government statistics, NGO research and policy material on reproductive health, child marriage, migration and labour protection. The evidence pointed toward connected responses: worker registration, safe accommodation, basic amenities, health and accident coverage, and stronger local employment.

## Why visibility matters

Invisible labour is easy to treat as an individual problem. Making it visible means tracing the institutions and economic arrangements that distribute risk. It also means recognising women workers as experts in the conditions they navigate, not simply subjects of concern.
